Experience, projects, and education answer different questions. Choose details for the role you want and the instructions in its application. There is no section order or work-history length that fits every employer, country, or career stage.

## Describe Work with Accurate Scope

For each relevant role, identify the employer or approved generic label, your actual title, dates, location when useful, and work evidence. Reverse chronological order is common and easy to follow, but a skills-first or combination format might help some candidates. Test the final exported file's reading order and follow a required employer format.

The [CareerOneStop work-experience guide](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/ResumeGuide/work-experience.aspx) recommends focusing on relevant tasks and accomplishments. The number of bullets and years of history depends on the posting and space available. A federal application, for example, may ask for different details than a private-sector resume.

**Fictional technical example:**

> Community Library, part-time systems assistant, 2024 to 2025
>
> Documented a test restore of the library's training database and reported two missing steps to the supervisor.

**Fictional nontechnical example:**

> Riverside Market, customer service associate, 2023 to 2025
>
> Reconciled the daily returns log and explained policy exceptions to the shift lead before closing.

Both examples state work a candidate could explain. The first does not claim production ownership. The second does not invent revenue or satisfaction figures. Use the [Module 4 claim-check worksheet](/resume-optimization-course/bullet-points-and-accomplishments/) for every result.

## Handle Gaps and Contract Work Without Oversharing

Show accurate dates in a consistent format. Follow the employer's required fields. You do not owe a resume reader private health, disability, caregiving, immigration, or family details. An optional neutral line such as "Career break, 2024" may help when you want to account for time. You may also leave a gap unexplained on the resume and prepare a brief truthful answer if asked.

List contract or freelance work under your real business or contractor relationship. Name a client only when the agreement and client permit it. "Confidential financial-services client" is safer than a real name when disclosure is restricted, but do not imply work for a client you did not serve. Group short projects when that makes dates and responsibilities clearer. Keep background-check forms consistent with the underlying employment record.

## Choose Projects by Evidence

A project belongs on the resume when it helps show a required skill and you can discuss your work. A local project with a reproducible README and test output can be useful even when it has no public deployment. A tutorial project can also show learning if you identify your changes and limits honestly. Do not describe a class exercise as a client implementation.

| Project field | Check |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What problem or learning goal did you choose? |
| Your contribution | Which parts did you build or test yourself? |
| Evidence | Is there an approved link, sanitized screenshot, README, or demonstration? |
| Verification | What test or review did you complete, and what failed? |
| Disclosure | Did you remove secrets, private data, and employer code? |

If you cannot share the repository, describe the work in terms your owner permits and keep the private evidence for interviews.

## Place Education for the Target Role

Include credentials the posting requests and label incomplete programs accurately. Give the institution, qualification, field, and completion date when relevant. Include GPA or coursework only when the employer asks or the detail supports the role and you are comfortable sharing it. Use the institution's official degree name where accuracy matters. Abbreviations are acceptable when clear.

| Candidate | Reasonable order | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fictional recent graduate with a relevant capstone | Education, project, then work | The capstone supplies the strongest role evidence. |
| Fictional experienced service technician | Work, licenses, then education | Recent troubleshooting and required license matter most. |

These are options, not rules. Compare the order with the specific posting and have another reader locate the requested evidence.

## Completeness and Privacy Check

Before moving to Module 7, check:

1. Titles, employers, dates, education, and project status are accurate.
2. Every bullet names your actual part, with no borrowed team result.
3. Work gaps use only detail you choose to disclose.
4. Client names, code, metrics, and links have permission for external sharing.
5. Every section supports the target job or a required application field.
6. The exported document has selectable text in the intended order.

## Practice and Pass Check

Create two layouts from the same sanitized facts: one for the fictional recent graduate and one for the experienced service technician above. Explain why each order places the most relevant evidence first. Then apply the same check to your private master resume. Ask a reader to locate the role, dates, one work example, one project or credential, and any missing or ambiguous claim. Repair the draft without inventing detail.
