A resume bullet helps a reader understand what you did, your part in the work, and a relevant result or scope. A duty is still useful when you lack a measured outcome. Do not add numbers or take sole credit for team work to make a sentence sound stronger.

## Start with a Claim You Can Explain

Use this pattern when it fits: action, object, your contribution, and observed result. If there is no measured result, state the scope, purpose, or handoff. The result does not need to be a percentage.

| Fictional starting note | Revised bullet | Evidence to check |
|---|---|---|
| Answered customer questions | Resolved product setup questions through the support queue and updated the setup FAQ after repeated issues | Ticket history and FAQ revision |
| Helped with a website | Contributed accessible form markup and tested keyboard navigation for the team's registration page | Pull request and test notes |
| Managed weekly stock | Reconciled weekly inventory counts and flagged mismatches for the store manager | Count sheets and manager handoff |
| Built an API | Built a test API for a portfolio project, documented its endpoints, and added error-case tests | Repository, README, and test output |

These are fictional examples. Do not copy a metric, employer, or project you cannot verify. In the second example, "contributed" correctly describes shared ownership. "Assisted" or "helped" also fits when it states your real role and the work remains clear.

## Use Numbers Only with a Basis

An exact count is useful when you have a record. An estimate needs a defensible method, period, and label. If you cannot reconstruct the number during an interview, omit it. You can still describe scale with a supported fact, such as "across three teams," when the team records support it.

| Claim | Defensible basis | Resume treatment |
|---|---|---|
| "Cut response time by 25%" | Dated before-and-after ticket report using the same measure | Use the measured percentage and period |
| "Handled about 30 cases a week" | Four representative weeks of your own queue totals | Label "about" and retain the calculation |
| "Saved $1 million" | No budget record or approved attribution | Remove the figure and describe the process change |
| "Led the migration" | Team charter names you as one contributor | Say "contributed to" and name your component |

The [CareerOneStop resume guide](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/JobSearch/Resumes/ResumeGuide/work-experience.aspx) recommends describing relevant tasks and accomplishments. Employer screening also varies by role and application. No bullet format guarantees an interview.

## Match Verb and Tense to the Work

Choose a verb you can defend: wrote, tested, coordinated, reviewed, resolved, built, or contributed. "Responsible for" can be clear when it names a continuing duty. A current role may contain past-tense bullets for completed projects and present-tense bullets for ongoing duties. Keep each bullet readable and follow the application's length or format limits.

## Claim-Check Worksheet

Before adding a bullet, complete one row privately:

| Field | Your record |
|---|---|
| Target job duty | Which posting requirement does this bullet address? |
| Action and contribution | What did you do yourself, and what did the team do? |
| Result or scope | What changed, or what work did you handle? |
| Source | Which ticket, document, project, supervisor, or record supports the claim? |
| Calculation | If you used a number, what period and denominator did you use? |
| Disclosure | Does the bullet expose a client, employer secret, personal data, or restricted metric? |
| Interview check | Can you explain the task, decision, limit, and result without private data? |

For a confidential project, describe the work at a level your employer permits. Replace protected details with a truthful generic description. Do not upload source records or an employer resume to an unapproved AI service.

## Practice and Pass Check

Rewrite three bullets from a sanitized sample resume or your own private draft. Include one individual contribution, one team contribution, and one duty without a measured result. Complete the claim-check worksheet for each. Ask a reader to identify your specific contribution and what evidence supports the claim. Revise any bullet that implies unsupported ownership, precision, or access to confidential information.
