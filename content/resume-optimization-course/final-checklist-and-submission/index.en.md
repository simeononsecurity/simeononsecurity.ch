The final step is a submission check. Follow the employer's instructions, verify your claims, and inspect the file you will send. A third-party match score is a local editing aid, not an employer pass mark.

______

## Choose Length and Format

Keep enough relevant history to support the role. One page works for some applicants. Two or more pages make sense when substantial relevant work needs space. A CV for academic or research roles follows different conventions. Cut repetitive or old material before shrinking the font.

Use the file type listed in the posting or application form. If both PDF and DOCX are accepted, check which version preserves readable text and formatting. Do not assume one format works best in every system. For example, [Greenhouse's resume parsing guidance](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse) describes supported file types and parsing limits. An employer might use a different system or custom settings.

Name the file clearly, such as `FirstLast_Resume.pdf`. Use the extension of the file you submitted. Avoid `final_v3` labels that add no value to the recipient.

______

## Submission Checklist

- [ ] I followed the posting's instructions on format, length, attachments, and naming.
- [ ] My email, phone, and links work, and I included only contact details I want to share.
- [ ] Dates, titles, credentials, tools, and results are accurate.
- [ ] Any estimate is labeled, and I have not invented a metric or responsibility.
- [ ] My strongest relevant evidence appears where a reader will find it.
- [ ] My skills section matches work, projects, or training I can explain.
- [ ] I checked spelling, grammar, and consistent date formatting.
- [ ] I copied text from the submitted file into a plain-text editor and checked its reading order.
- [ ] I opened the file on another device or browser and checked the visible layout.
- [ ] I checked the application form's parsed fields and corrected errors before submitting.
- [ ] I saved a copy of the posting and the exact file I sent.

If the employer asks for a cover letter or a different document, add those items to your own checklist. If the form accepts a file but fails to extract a field, correct the form and consider a simpler export. Do not infer a hidden rejection rule from a tool's percentage.

______

## Common Revisions

| Draft | Clearer option |
|---|---|
| `Responsible for reports` | Name the report, your contribution, and its use |
| `Helped improve onboarding` | Explain which step you changed and what followed |
| `Expert in Python` | List a Python project and the part you wrote |
| `Reduced delays by 40%` with no records | Describe the change without a number, or label a documented estimate |
| A creative section title | Use a familiar title such as `Experience` or `Projects` |
| A long list of unrelated tools | Keep the tools relevant to the target work |

Use verbs that match your role. `Led` is wrong if you contributed under another person's lead. A date gap does not require a story in every resume. Follow the job and local hiring conventions for personal details, photos, and academic information.

______

## Final Exercise

Choose one posting. Save its instructions, export your resume in an accepted format, and complete the checklist above. Ask another reader to compare your top three claims against the evidence in your resume. Fix each mismatch, then save the submitted version and application date.
