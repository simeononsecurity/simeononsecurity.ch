A job posting gives you useful vocabulary. Use its terms when they describe work you have done. No universal ATS score or exact keyword count determines the result. Employers configure screening, search, and review differently.

By the end of this module, you will have a role-specific skills section and two bullets that support your strongest skills.

______

## Start with the Posting

1. Save the posting with the job title and date. The employer might change or remove it.
2. Mark required skills, preferred skills, duties, and industry terms separately. Repetition alone does not make a skill mandatory.
3. Make a two-column list: terms from the posting and evidence from your work, study, or projects. Leave a blank when you lack evidence.
4. Choose the terms with both relevance and evidence. Do not copy a requirement you have not met.

| Posting term | Your evidence | Resume action |
|---|---|---|
| Python automation | Wrote a script to check 140 CSV records | Add a project bullet with the task and verified count |
| Kubernetes | Read a tutorial only | Do not claim production experience |
| Incident response | Triaged alerts during a supervised rotation | Describe the rotation and your role |

Job titles and acronyms vary. If you used a tool or method named in the posting, use its correct name. Do not change an official job title. A clarifying parenthesis is appropriate when it stays accurate, such as `Support Engineer (customer-facing platform support)`.

______

## Write the Skills Section

Group relevant skills by function. Keep the list short enough to scan. Remove tools you cannot discuss with concrete examples.

```text
Languages: Python, SQL
Cloud and systems: AWS EC2, Linux
Operations: Incident triage, log analysis
```

A skill list helps a reader find terms. Your experience and project bullets show what you did with them. For example:

> Used Python and SQL to compare 140 exported records with the source system. Found 12 mismatches and documented the correction steps.

The counts above are an example. Use your own verified figures. If you lack a measured result, explain the delivered artifact or change instead.

Spell out an acronym when the audience might not know it, then use the short form. Add an alternate term only when both names accurately describe your work. Placement at the top of a resume does not confer a known universal ranking advantage.

______

## Check the File and the Claim

1. Export the format accepted in the posting.
2. Open the exported file, select its text, and paste it into a plain-text editor.
3. Check that the skills and bullets appear in reading order and remain readable.
4. Ask a second person to compare your evidence against the posting. Remove any claim you cannot defend in an interview.

The [ATS Resume Improver](https://atsresumeimprover.netlify.app/) offers a local comparison of your text and a posting. Its gap labels and percentages are editing prompts. They do not represent an employer's screening threshold or predict an interview. Review suggestions before accepting them, since a missing term might reflect experience you do not have.

______

## Practice

Use one real posting and a copy of your resume. Produce a three-row evidence table like the one above, a revised skills section, and two supporting bullets. Check the exported text. You are done when another reader can point to the evidence for every listed skill and you can explain each claim.
