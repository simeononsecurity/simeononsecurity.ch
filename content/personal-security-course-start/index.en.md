**This course treats the individual as the asset.** Each lesson applies security decisions to personal accounts, devices, money, relationships, and physical access.

The difference matters. You do not have a security team, a managed detection service, or a compliance officer. You have a limited amount of time and a threat model shaped by who you are. **The course is built around the constraint.**

*Every module ends with something you produce: a decision, a configuration, or a record. The exercise turns the lesson into evidence you review later.*

## What You Will Learn

The course runs in a fixed order, from account credentials up to the physical layer. Each module builds on the previous one, and the ordering is deliberate rather than alphabetical.

| Module | Focus | Evidence you keep |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Authentication** | Credentials and second factors | Redacted factor, recovery, and session test |
| **2. Web Browsing** | Tracking and browser exposure | Settings audit, isolation test, and breakage note |
| **3. Email** | The recovery gateway | Rules, grants, alias, remote-content, and authentication test |
| **4. Messaging** | Conversation privacy | Contact, linked-device, timer, and recovery test |
| **5. Social Media** | Public exposure | Outside profile view, session audit, and export route |
| **6. Networks** | Traffic and routing | Router backup and segmentation test matrix |
| **7. Mobile Devices** | Sensors and tracking | Permission, backup, privileged-access, and carrier audit |
| **8. Personal Computers** | The operating system | Encryption, recovery-key, restore, and service evidence |
| **9. Smart Home** | Microphones and cameras | Inventory, data-flow sketch, isolation test, and removal trigger |
| **10. Personal Finance** | Money and identity | Alert test, freeze status, recovery, and dispute routes |
| **11. Human Aspect** | Social engineering | Three role-play results and verified callback routes |
| **12. Physical Security** | The world outside the screen | Lock test, spare-device recovery test, and disposal choice |

## How This Course Differs From a Compliance Checklist

A compliance checklist asks whether a control exists. **This course asks whether a control changes your outcome**, which is a different question with a different answer.

Three commitments shape every module:

- **Priority is explicit.** Items carry an impact ranking, so you know what to do first when time is short.
- **Trade-offs are named.** Every recommendation carries its cost, because advice without cost is marketing.
- **Tools are optional.** Where a client-side tool helps, the module links one. None require an account or an upload.

*Backups and browser fingerprint controls solve different problems. The relevant modules test each control against its stated outcome.*

## What You Need Before Starting

**No technical background is required.** The course assumes you use a phone, a computer, and a handful of online accounts. Modules 6 and 8 go deeper for readers who manage their own hardware, and each flags where a step becomes optional.

| Requirement | Why |
|---|---|
| **A password manager** | Module 1 depends on one, and later modules assume it exists |
| **Several short work sessions** | Recovery checks deserve time, a trusted device, and a safe stopping point |
| **Willingness to review defaults** | Each module asks you to inspect current settings before changing them |
| **Budget awareness** | The course starts with built-in and free options, then names trade-offs for paid services |

*Work in order the first time. Modules 11 and 12 assume the habits established in Part One.*

## Safe Working Method

Use a supported, updated device and the official provider settings page. Menu labels differ across Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, browsers, carriers, routers, banks, and account providers.

Before each module:

1. **Record the starting state.** Save a configuration export, screenshot, or redacted note where the system offers one.
2. **Change one item.** Test the normal task before moving forward.
3. **Keep recovery available.** Do not remove an old factor, route, device, or network until the replacement passes.
4. **Pause safely.** Stop with the prior control still active, the device connected, and the next action recorded.
5. **Protect evidence.** Course records contain no passwords, recovery codes, full account numbers, or identity documents.

> **Active compromise:** use a trusted device to revoke unknown sessions, secure recovery accounts, change exposed credentials, and contact the provider or financial institution. Check breach history after control returns to you.

If an employer, school, family administrator, carrier, landlord, or financial institution controls the setting, document the restriction. Choose a control within your authority, such as session review, alerts, a separate personal account, or an official support request.

## Final Review

Completion requires evidence from all twelve modules. A filled template alone does not pass.

1. Re-read your threat model and name the three harms you prioritize.
2. Select one record from each module and confirm its date, observed result, and recovery route.
3. Run the authentication session-revocation test, computer restore test, network isolation test, financial alert test, human verification role-play, and spare-device physical test again.
4. Mark failed checks as open work. Record an owner, next action, and review date.
5. Ask whether any control creates a lockout, safety, accessibility, employment, school, family, or legal problem. Adjust the control and retest.

**Pass gate**

- [ ] Twelve redacted records identify observed evidence
- [ ] Six capstone retests pass or have a dated correction plan
- [ ] Recovery works without exposing secrets in the course notes
- [ ] Controls fit the learner's threat model, authority, safety, and accessibility needs
- [ ] Every provider-specific instruction includes a current source or check date

## Retest Schedule

Repeat the six capstone tests after a security incident, provider migration, recovery change, new primary device, or router replacement. Set a review date no more than twelve months away for unchanged records. Shorten the interval for accounts or devices tied to a current threat.
