Engineering manager duties vary by organization size, reporting structure, product, jurisdiction, and technical needs. Compare the actual role with individual contributor work before moving tracks.

## What Engineering Managers Actually Do

An engineering manager might own people processes, delivery, hiring, stakeholder work, or technical decisions. HR and legal teams retain duties defined by policy and law.

| Responsibility | What It Involves |
|---|---|
| **1:1 meetings** | Private check-ins at an agreed cadence for support, feedback, goals, and concerns |
| **Hiring** | Writing job descriptions, reviewing resumes, running interviews, making offers |
| **Performance management** | Setting expectations, delivering feedback, documenting growth and performance risks |
| **Team planning** | Sprint planning, roadmap collaboration with PM, capacity planning |
| **Stakeholder management** | Communicating team progress, blockers, and risks to product, design, and leadership |
| **Conflict resolution** | Addressing interpersonal issues, differing technical opinions, and organizational friction |
| **Career development** | Helping engineers identify growth areas and create paths to promotion |

Some EM roles include regular coding or technical leadership. Others focus on people and delivery. Ask who owns architecture, performance decisions, compensation, staffing, incidents, and production changes.

## Is Engineering Management Right for You?

Ask yourself honestly:

- Do you find more satisfaction in improving someone else's output than in your own code?
- Can you have hard conversations (underperformance, behavioral issues, missed promotions) without avoiding them?
- Are you comfortable having less direct control over technical outcomes?
- Do meetings and process work energize you, or do they drain you?

*If the answer to any of these is "that sounds terrible," stay on the IC track. Both tracks are respected and well-compensated at senior levels.*

## Transitioning Into Engineering Management

An internal trial, external management hire, delivery role, technical EM route, or manager-of-managers route might fit. Verify training, authority, HR support, workload, and return-to-IC options.

A staged transition:

1. **Tech lead role**: coordinate technical work for a small team while continuing to code. Observe whether the coordination work energizes or drains you.
2. **Interim or acting EM**: step in for a manager on leave or for a team without a manager. Real trial run.
3. **Full EM role**: formally transition with explicit scope of responsibility, headcount, and clear success metrics.

For an external move, compare each employer's stated management experience and technical expectations.

## The Career Ladder Beyond Engineering Manager

| Level | Title | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **EM** | Engineering Manager | Manages a team or defined function under employer-specific scope |
| **Sr. EM** | Senior Engineering Manager | Manages a larger team or multiple junior managers |
| **Director** | Director of Engineering | Manages multiple teams, owns a domain or product area |
| **VP** | VP of Engineering | Manages a department, owns engineering strategy for a product or business unit |
| **SVP / CTO** | SVP Engineering or CTO | Companywide engineering leadership |

Director-and-above roles require demonstrated business acumen, not just people management skill. Budgeting, headcount planning, and board-level communication become part of the job.

## Staying Technical as an EM

Choose technical involvement which supports the team without overriding clear decision ownership.

- **Read code weekly**: review pull requests on your team. You do not have to write code to understand what is being built.
- **Participate in design reviews**: contribute questions and context, not directives.
- **Stay current in your domain**: follow the major developments in your team's technical stack.
- **Be honest about your limits**: track which skills you still practice and ask the appropriate specialist to review decisions outside your current depth.

Follow your organization's HR, legal, accessibility, compensation, privacy, labor, and performance-management policies. Keep sensitive personnel information out of course exercises.

## Practice and Verify

**Artifact: Management readiness scenario.** Compare an IC week with an engineering-manager week. Shadow with consent. Score readiness for coaching, delivery, hiring, performance process, accommodations, compensation, conflict, incidents, layoffs, and return-to-IC options. Write a first-90-day plan.

> **Expected result:** The decision names organization type, authority, support, legal or HR boundary, and a reversible next step.

```text

Decision: remain IC. Next step: shadow two one-to-ones with consent. Gap: performance-process training. Review: 60 days.
```

**Pass checklist**

- [ ] The artifact uses dated, role-specific evidence
- [ ] Claims, ownership, team credit, and unknown values are truthful
- [ ] Privacy, secrets, licenses, accessibility, cost, and safety received review where relevant
- [ ] A reviewer or repeatable check produced feedback
- [ ] The record names a timed next action and checkpoint
