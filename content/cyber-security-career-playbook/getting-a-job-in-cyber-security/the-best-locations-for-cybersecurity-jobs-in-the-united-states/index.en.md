There is no single best U.S. city for every cybersecurity job seeker. Role, work authorization, clearance eligibility, local employers, housing costs, and family needs change the answer. This guide shows how to compare places with dated data instead of treating a job-board count as a stable ranking.

## Start with a Defined Occupation

The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) information security analyst profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm) describes one occupation, not every cybersecurity title. BLS's [May 2025 state wage tables](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oessrcst.htm) and [metropolitan area tables](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oessrcma.htm) give employment and wage estimates for defined areas. Read the table notes and match the duties of your target role to the occupation before using a number.

A higher local employment estimate does not equal more current vacancies. It measures workers employed at the data date. Search recent employer postings separately and record your search date, keywords, location filter, and duplicate-removal rule.

## Compare Three Candidate Regions

| Measure | What to record | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| BLS employment and wage | Occupation code, area, May 2025 estimate, median annual wage | Covers workers already employed, across experience levels |
| Current openings | Employer, title, posting date, exact work location | Listings can repeat or stay online after a role closes |
| Living costs | Housing, transport, taxes, and [BEA state or metro regional price parity](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area) | A regional price index does not describe your household budget |
| Eligibility | Clearance, citizenship, license, onsite work, travel | A strong local market does not waive a hard requirement |
| Career fit | Team, mentoring, shift, training, and advancement | Job quality differs within the same city |

The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes regional price parities for states and metropolitan areas. Record the geography and year for each figure. Use current housing quotes for the actual neighborhood you might choose.

## Example Search Plan

You might compare Washington, D.C., Austin, and Raleigh because you have family or job leads in those regions. Those are examples to research, not a top-three ranking.

{{< figure src="Washington DC.webp" alt="Illustration of Washington, D.C., as one example region to research" >}}

For each region, collect five postings for the same role. Record whether the employer requires onsite work, a clearance, or a location-specific schedule. Then compare BLS area wages and your own expected expenses. A role with a higher listed salary might leave less after housing and travel, or offer better benefits. Calculate both scenarios using real offers when available.

## Practice

Create a comparison table for three places you would consider living. Include source URL, observation date, occupation definition, wage measure, posting count method, expected housing and transport cost, and eligibility. Explain which location you would investigate first and which missing data might change your choice. Recheck the table before relocating or accepting an offer.
