Professional networking works when you **learn, contribute, and follow through**. A contact is a person, not a shortcut to a referral.

## Protect Your Privacy

Before posting, review your profile, old posts, contact details, location, employer references, and public activity. Remove customer names, internal systems, badges with identifiers, tickets, private screenshots, tokens, and home-lab addresses tied to your real network.

Do not open unknown files or run code from a community post. Report harassment, impersonation, malware links, and payment requests through the platform's process.

## Choose a Community

Review the current rules, moderation, cost, ownership, and recent activity before joining. Favor spaces tied to a local professional group, training program, open-source project, conference, or defensive practice community.

Read first. Identify which questions fit the group and how moderators handle sensitive material.

## Make Small Contributions

Beginner-friendly work includes:

- Reproducing a documentation step and reporting the exact mismatch
- Fixing a typo or broken reference after reading contribution rules
- Writing a summary of an official update with a source date
- Reviewing a fictional incident record for clarity
- Helping another learner reproduce a safe lab result
- Volunteering for event check-in, notes, or accessibility support

Large security tool codebases are optional. They are not a required starting point.

## Send a Focused Message

```text
Hello Alex. Your talk on alert triage helped me separate observations from assumptions in my lab report. I revised one synthetic case and linked the redacted result below. If you have time, which field would you expect a junior analyst to add before escalation? No reply is needed if your schedule is full.
```

Ask one narrow question. Do not attach files without consent. Follow up once after a reasonable interval, then stop.

## Four-Week Exercise

| Week | Action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Review privacy and join one moderated community | Profile checklist and saved rules |
| **2** | Ask one researched question and answer one question with a source | Links and correction notes |
| **3** | Make one small contribution | Issue, pull request, event task, or reviewed artifact |
| **4** | Request one focused career or artifact review | Consent, feedback, and revision |

Keep a private contact log with date, context, promised follow-up, consent, and result. Do not store sensitive personal details.

**Pass condition:** Complete four useful interactions, deliver every promised follow-up, publish no sensitive data, and revise one artifact from feedback. Referral requests do not count as contributions.

## Next Step

Bring a finished artifact to your next conversation. The [Interview Guide](/cyber-security-career-playbook/getting-a-job-in-cyber-security/cyber-security-interview-questions/) shows how to explain the evidence.
