There is no single entry-level cybersecurity market. Role title, duties, location, employer, work authorization, clearance, schedule, and prior IT expectations change the search.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks **information security analysts** as one U.S. occupation. Its education, wage, and outlook data do not describe every junior security title. Use current postings for your target market.

## Build a Job Sample

Collect 20 postings over two weeks. Keep one role family and location rule. Record:

- Employer and posting date
- Role title and seniority
- Location, remote eligibility, travel, and schedule
- Required and preferred experience
- Repeated tasks
- Education, certification, work authorization, or clearance filters
- Salary range where disclosed
- Application date and result

Count requirements. Do not convert your sample into a national claim.

## Build One Work Sample

Choose the most common task which fits a safe synthetic exercise. For an analyst path, create a fictional alert report with:

1. Alert and asset context
2. Observed evidence
3. Two possible explanations
4. One least-disruptive check
5. Severity rationale
6. Escalation threshold
7. Limitations

Use reserved example addresses and invented names. Do not upload employer logs, customer data, or challenge solutions which violate platform rules.

## Apply by Fit

Group roles into three bands:

- **Strong match:** You meet the filters and show evidence for most repeated tasks.
- **Stretch:** You meet the filters and lack one or two trainable skills.
- **Blocked:** You lack a stated legal, location, clearance, education, or experience condition with no alternative.

Spend most time on strong matches. Apply to selected stretch roles. Skip blocked roles unless the employer names an accepted substitute you hold.

## Review the Funnel

After 20 qualified applications, calculate:

- Screening response rate
- Interview rate
- Stage where rejection happens
- Repeated missing requirement
- Source of the posting

No responses often point to targeting or resume evidence. Early interviews followed by rejection often point to explanation or task gaps. Later rejection needs role-specific feedback where available. These are hypotheses. Test one change at a time.

## Four-Week Exercise

1. Collect 20 matched postings.
2. Build one artifact tied to the top repeated task.
3. Ask one reviewer to score accuracy, safety, clarity, and reproducibility.
4. Submit ten strong-match applications with tailored evidence.
5. Review results and change one variable.

**Pass condition:** Your tracker has complete dates and role constraints, the artifact contains no sensitive data, one reviewer checked it, and your next action follows observed funnel evidence.
