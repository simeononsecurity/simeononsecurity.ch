Tools, threats, and employer needs change. No unsupported decade comparison tells you which skill to learn next. Use a repeatable review: define a target role, check current primary sources, practice one safe task, and record the result.

## Start with the Work Role

[NIST's NICE Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/applied-cybersecurity/nice/nice-framework-resource-center) gives a shared language for cybersecurity tasks and knowledge. Compare one NICE work role with five current local postings. Mark which tasks appear in both and where the employer uses a different title or scope.

A certification might matter when a posting names it. Read its current objectives and eligibility rules before paying. CISSP, for example, has experience requirements that differ from beginner credentials. Do not assume a certificate or portfolio produces a fixed hiring outcome.

## Read a Current Technical Source

Use the [CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) as one source for vulnerabilities with evidence of exploitation. A catalog entry is a reason to check your own software inventory and vendor guidance, not proof that every system is affected. Record the CVE, affected product and versions, date, official remediation, and whether the product exists in your authorized environment.

For control planning, the [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) supplies outcome categories. It does not replace an applicable law, customer contract, or risk decision. If you work with privacy or sector rules, check the jurisdiction and the actual service before stating an obligation.

## Practice a Weekly Review

1. Choose one role and one source item relevant to it.
2. Write down what the source states and what remains uncertain for your environment.
3. In a disposable lab or on a system you administer, check an inventory or configuration without scanning third-party targets.
4. Record your observation, next check, proposed change, approval needed, and verification method.
5. Explain the result to another learner in plain language. Correct any claim that goes beyond your evidence.

For example, a vendor advisory might name a vulnerable software version. A defensible note says your test VM runs a different version and shows the command output. It does not claim your whole organization is safe.

## Career Check

Every few months, compare your practice record with the duties in current postings. Keep useful evidence in a portfolio only after removing secrets, personal data, and employer details. A CTF or lab writeup should name its authorized target and limit. Seek feedback on your explanations as well as your commands.
