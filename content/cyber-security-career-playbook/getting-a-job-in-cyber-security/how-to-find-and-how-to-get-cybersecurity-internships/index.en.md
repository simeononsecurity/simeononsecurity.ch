An internship gives you supervised practice when its duties and feedback are clear. It does not guarantee a permanent job. Read each posting for eligibility, pay, schedule, location, and the work you will be allowed to perform.

## Find Current Openings

Search your school career center, employer career pages, and job boards using terms such as `security operations intern`, `GRC intern`, and `IT security intern`. Save the posting date and apply through the employer's official process. In the United States, [USAJOBS Early Careers](https://www.usajobs.gov/EarlyCareers) lists federal student and recent-graduate hiring paths. Check each vacancy's citizenship, enrollment, clearance, and deadline rules.

A program name in an old article does not prove the program is open now. Verify the current posting and hiring path before preparing an application.


## Compare the Internship Terms

| Item | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Work | Will you review alerts, document controls, write scripts, or shadow staff? |
| Supervision | Who approves changes and handles incidents beyond your authority? |
| Learning | Will you receive feedback and a defined project or rotation? |
| Pay and expenses | What is the wage, travel cost, equipment policy, and payment schedule? |
| Eligibility | Is enrollment, academic credit, citizenship, or location required? |
| End date | Is the role fixed-term? What would a later application require? |

Do not assume an unpaid internship is lawful or worthwhile because it offers academic credit. In the United States, the [Department of Labor's Fact Sheet 71](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/71-flsa-internships) describes the fact-specific primary-beneficiary test for unpaid internships at for-profit employers. State and local rules also matter. Ask your school and an appropriate local authority about the terms before accepting unpaid work.

## Prepare One Work Sample

Use a disposable lab and synthetic data. For example, create a short alert triage note with a sample timestamp, observed event, two possible explanations, a next check, and an escalation condition. State what you did yourself. Do not scan an employer, collect real account data, or claim that lab practice was production incident response.

Tailor the resume to the actual duties. A class project, help-desk role, volunteer task, or home lab might supply relevant evidence. A certification is useful when the posting asks for it, but no standard bundle of languages or tools fits every internship.

## Application Exercise

Collect three current internship postings. Fill the terms table for each and mark one required skill you can demonstrate. Write a two-sentence explanation of your work sample and a question about supervision. Ask another person to verify your links, dates, and eligibility before submitting. Track each application and response.
