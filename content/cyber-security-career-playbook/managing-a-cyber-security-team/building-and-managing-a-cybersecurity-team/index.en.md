Start with **business services and risk**, then decide which work must happen, when it must happen, and who owns each decision. A list of tools or certifications is not a staffing plan.

## Define Security Services

List critical business services, data, legal and contract needs, operating hours, dependencies, and major failure effects. Translate them into security services such as identity, vulnerability management, detection, incident response, application security, risk, awareness, and third-party assurance.

For each service, record:

- Owner and decision authority
- Customers and required outcome
- Intake route and priority rule
- Coverage hours and response target
- Required evidence
- Dependencies and backup owner
- Quality and risk measures

## Map Work Roles

Use the current NICE Framework Components to describe work. NICE Work Roles group responsibilities. They do not equal job titles or staffing counts.

Build a work-role matrix:

| Service | Recurring tasks | Primary owner | Reviewer | Coverage | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alert triage** | Validate, scope, record, escalate | Analyst | Incident lead | 24 by 7 or stated hours | Internal, managed, or mixed |
| **Identity review** | Review access, exceptions, evidence | IAM engineer | System owner | Scheduled | Internal or mixed |
| **Risk acceptance** | Present evidence and options | Risk owner | Executive authority | As needed | Retained internally |

## Design Capacity

Estimate recurring work, projects, leave, training, on-call recovery, and surge response. Separate headcount from coverage. One person does not provide continuous coverage.

Protect separation of duties for approval, implementation, and review where risk requires it. Name temporary exceptions and review dates.

## Choose a Sourcing Mix

Use internal staff, shared IT staff, contractors, managed providers, or a mixed model. Retain a named organizational owner for risk, vendor oversight, incident decisions, and evidence access.

## Staffing Exercise

Create a 90-day plan for a fictional 200-person organization with one public service, regulated customer data, business-hours support, and an external monitoring provider.

Deliver:

1. Five security services
2. Work-role matrix
3. Coverage and on-call plan
4. Sourcing decisions
5. Three measures
6. Two risks and owners
7. Hiring or training priority

**Pass condition:** Every critical service has an owner, backup, coverage rule, evidence output, and escalation. No vendor task lacks internal accountability.
