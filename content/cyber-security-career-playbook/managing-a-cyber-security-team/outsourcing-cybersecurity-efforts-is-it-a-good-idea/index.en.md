Outsourcing fits some security work when the organization defines the outcome, keeps an accountable owner, and verifies provider performance. A credential list or encryption claim does not prove service quality.

## Define the Outcome

State the service, systems, coverage, response target, evidence, exclusions, dependencies, and internal owner. Compare providers against the same statement.

## Review the Provider

Check:

- Experience with your service, scale, and obligations
- Audit report scope, period, exceptions, and subservice organizations
- Staff access, least privilege, logging, and separation
- Data types, locations, retention, deletion, and subprocessors
- Incident detection, notice, evidence, communications, and cooperation
- Service levels, maintenance, and escalation
- Continuity, insurance, concentration, and financial risk
- Right to audit, regulator access, and contract changes
- Export, transition, deletion, and termination support

Certifications and reports support due diligence. Read their scope and exceptions.

## Write Shared Responsibilities

Create a RACI for alerts, severity, containment, customer notice, evidence preservation, recovery, detection changes, and service closure. Name one accountable internal role for each decision.

## Test the Contract

Run a tabletop with provider outage, late breach notice, disputed severity, missing logs, and contract termination. Record which clause, owner, and fallback handles each event.

**Pass condition:** The service has measurable outcomes, direct evidence access, named incident duties, approved subprocessors, continuity, and a tested exit path. Unresolved high-impact gaps block selection.
