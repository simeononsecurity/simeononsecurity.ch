Choose a course after you choose a **target role and missing skill**. Course completion is evidence of study. Employers still need work samples, experience, or an assessment aligned with their tasks.

## Start With Foundations

Most technical security paths depend on four foundations:

- **Operating systems:** users, permissions, processes, services, files, logs, and updates
- **Networking:** addressing, DNS, routing, ports, TLS, packet flow, and segmentation
- **Scripting:** input, output, errors, files, APIs, secrets, and tests
- **Security practice:** identity, hardening, vulnerability handling, detection, response, recovery, and risk communication

Review three target postings. Mark each repeated task as ready, needs practice, or unknown. Select one unknown which appears in at least two postings.

## Follow a Role Route

| Target route | Learn first | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| **Security operations analyst** | Networking, Windows or Linux logs, alert triage, incident records | A synthetic alert report with observations, scope, severity, and escalation |
| **Security engineer** | Systems administration, networking, automation, identity, change control | A hardening change with test evidence and rollback |
| **Governance, risk, and compliance** | Business processes, risk, control design, policy, audit evidence | A fictional control record with owner, test, evidence, and gap |
| **Cloud security** | One cloud platform, identity, networking, logging, infrastructure configuration, cost controls | A diagram and read-only posture review with cleanup record |
| **Application security** | One language, HTTP, source control, testing, software design | A small code review with a fixed defect and regression test |
| **Operational technology security** | Networking, industrial processes, safety, asset management, change limits | A passive architecture review with safety and availability constraints |

Offensive training needs written authorization, a defined scope, and disposable targets. Keep vulnerable machines on isolated host-only networks. Never scan public addresses or a school, employer, or home network without permission.

## Evaluate a Course

Record these fields before paying:

1. **Exact course and version**
2. **Verification date**
3. **Prerequisites**
4. **Full price**, including labs, exams, renewals, equipment, and cloud use
5. **Access period** and cancellation terms
6. **Instructor or reviewer access**
7. **Lab isolation and reset process**
8. **Saved learner output**
9. **Assessment method**
10. **Accessibility needs**

Do not rely on a provider's broad catalog claim. Open the exact course page, syllabus, terms, and refund policy.

## Use Free Primary Material

Primary references support a low-cost route:

- NIST publishes the **NICE Framework** for work roles, tasks, knowledge, and skills.
- NIST publishes the **Cybersecurity Framework 2.0** for managing cybersecurity outcomes.
- CISA publishes defensive learning resources and the **Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog**.
- Vendors publish operating system, cloud, identity, and network documentation.

Free reading alone does not build performance. Pair one concept with one bounded task and one reviewed artifact.

## Four-Week Selection Exercise

**Week 1:** Select one role. Compare three postings. Name one repeated missing skill.

**Week 2:** Compare three courses with the evaluation fields above. Include one free route and one paid route.

**Week 3:** Complete a small sample lesson or official tutorial. Save an artifact with starting state, action, result, and limits.

**Week 4:** Ask a practitioner, instructor, or peer to score the artifact for accuracy, clarity, safety, and reproducibility. Revise once.

**Pass condition:** Your decision names one target role, cites three dated postings, compares full costs, verifies prerequisites, and identifies the exact artifact you will produce. The selected course must close a repeated gap. A course which offers no relevant practice or review fails the check.

## Stop Buying Courses

Pause new purchases until you finish the selected output, receive feedback, and test the skill against another scenario. Buy another course only when a new repeated job requirement or observed performance gap supports the expense.
