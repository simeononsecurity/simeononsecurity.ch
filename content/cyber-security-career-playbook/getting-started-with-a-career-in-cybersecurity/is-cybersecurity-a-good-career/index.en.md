Cybersecurity work includes alert triage, security engineering, governance, education, incident response, and testing. The schedules, responsibilities, and entry requirements differ. Choose a role by the work you want to do and the opportunities open to you.

## Read the Data Carefully

The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics information security analyst profile](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm) reports a May 2025 median annual wage of $129,180 for workers in that occupation and projects 21% employment growth from 2025 to 2035. The median includes employed people at different experience levels. The projection is national, not a count of entry-level openings or an estimate for each security specialty.

Automation changes some tasks, but that BLS profile does not prove a fixed fall in junior hiring or that one security role is immune to AI tools. Compare current local postings and ask employers what humans review, approve, and escalate.

## Match Work to Your Interests

| Type of work | Sample question | Practice task |
|---|---|---|
| Security operations | Do you like testing an alert against logs and reporting uncertainty? | Triage three synthetic login events |
| Governance and risk | Do you like explaining a control and documenting an exception? | Write a risk entry for a fictional service |
| Security engineering | Do you like configuring and verifying system controls? | Set access rules in a disposable lab |
| Authorized testing | Do you like documenting a finding and a reproducible fix? | Test a deliberately vulnerable local target with permission |

Security roles also include customer contact, paperwork, maintenance windows, and occasional urgent work. Ask about on-call duties, mentoring, and decision authority before accepting a position.

## Compare the Cost of Entry

Read five recent postings for one role in your area. Mark required and preferred experience, credentials, education, work location, and clearance eligibility. A certificate helps when the employer asks for it, but it does not guarantee an interview. A home lab is practice evidence, not a substitute for a hard legal or employer requirement.

Estimate tuition, exam fees, lab costs, and study time for two possible routes. Use [the bootcamp comparison](/cyber-security-career-playbook/cyber-security-certifications-degrees-and-certificates/are-bootcamps-worth-it/) if you are considering a paid program. Use [the salary lesson](/cyber-security-career-playbook/getting-started-with-a-career-in-cybersecurity/cybersecurity-analyst-salaries-entry-level-to-pro/) for dated wages rather than unsourced role bands.

## Career-Fit Exercise

Choose one task in the table. Complete it in an authorized lab with synthetic data. Record starting state, evidence, your conclusion, an alternative explanation, and a next check. Show the record to someone else and ask them to distinguish observation from inference. Then interview a practitioner about the parts of their work your lab did not simulate.

Cybersecurity is a reasonable path when the work, schedule, learning cost, and local openings fit your goals. Revisit the choice as roles and circumstances change.
