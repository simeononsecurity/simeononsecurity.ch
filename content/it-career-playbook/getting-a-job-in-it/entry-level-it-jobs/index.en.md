Your first IT role might involve user support, endpoint setup, account administration, field service, or network operations. Employers combine these tasks in different ways. Read each posting for actual duties, schedule, location, and required experience before choosing a path.

The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics profile for computer support specialists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/computer-support-specialists.htm) is a national occupation measure, not a forecast for your city or a guarantee of a job. Its current projection covers 2025 to 2035. Check local postings for the skills and pay relevant to you.

## Compare Four Entry Paths

| Path | Typical work | Evidence to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Service desk | Intake, account access, endpoint troubleshooting, escalation | A clear ticket record and a safe account-recovery scenario |
| Field support | Device setup, network checks, user handoff | A setup checklist and a fault-isolation record |
| Managed service provider | Support across client environments | A customer-safe runbook and notes on access boundaries |
| Junior operations | Monitoring, backups, changes, escalation | A sample alert triage and a restore verification record |

A certificate might satisfy one employer's requirement and be optional at another. A home lab gives you evidence of practice, but employers also value paid work, internships, volunteer support, and school projects. Do not present a lab as production experience.

## Build One Interview Story

Use a disposable virtual machine or a device you own. Do not test an employer or public system without permission.

1. Record the starting state. For example, a Linux VM resolves `example.com` and reaches a local test service.
2. Introduce one safe fault, such as a wrong DNS resolver in the VM's network settings. Keep the original setting in your notes.
3. Note the user symptom, the command or UI check you used, and the observed result. Avoid recording real passwords or customer data.
4. Restore the setting. Repeat the check and record the outcome.
5. Write a one-page ticket: symptom, scope, evidence, hypothesis, change, verification, and what you would escalate.

A useful result says exactly what you saw. For example, `the VM reached the service by IP but failed to resolve its name, then resolved it after the DNS setting was restored`. The symptom alone does not prove the cause. Show the test that separated DNS from connectivity.

## Read and Apply to Real Postings

For each role, mark requirements as required, preferred, or unclear. Apply when you meet the essential duties and can explain gaps honestly. There is no universal percentage threshold. A posting might require a license, shift schedule, clearance, or onsite work that skills alone do not replace.

Prepare a short example for troubleshooting, customer communication, documentation, and escalation. Match the resume to the role without inventing duties or metrics. Ask the employer how it trains new staff and handles tickets that exceed an entry-level technician's authority.

Track applications by date, employer, role, requirements, contact, and next step. If response rates remain low after a reasonable sample, ask someone to review your resume and whether the roles match your evidence. Do not assume AI automation or one missing credential explains every rejection.
