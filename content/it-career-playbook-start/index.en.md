Information technology includes user support, systems administration, networking, cloud operations, security, and management. This playbook helps you choose a role, practice the work, and compare credentials and job requirements. It does not promise a job, salary, or certification result.

## What You Will Produce

- A target-role sheet based on five current local postings.
- A documented troubleshooting exercise with an observed result and verified fix.
- A training plan with current requirements and costs.
- A resume or portfolio claim linked to evidence you can explain.
- A choice of technical or management development goals if you already work in IT.

The lessons use U.S. examples unless a page states otherwise. Check your local rules, employer requirements, and current vendor exam pages before acting on time-sensitive details.

## Suggested Path

### 1. Choose a role

Read [career fit](/it-career-playbook/getting-started-in-it/is-it-a-good-career/) and [specializations](/it-career-playbook/getting-started-in-it/it-career-specializations/). Find five recent postings in your location. Mark duties, required skills, work schedule, and education requirements. Use the [salary lesson](/it-career-playbook/getting-started-in-it/it-salary-and-job-outlook/) to compare a defined BLS occupation with local listings. A national median covers workers at all experience levels, not a first-job offer.

### 2. Practice safely

Use a device you own or a disposable virtual machine. Follow [the home lab lesson](/it-career-playbook/getting-started-in-it/building-an-it-home-lab/) and the DNS fault exercise in [entry-level IT jobs](/it-career-playbook/getting-a-job-in-it/entry-level-it-jobs/). Record starting state, symptom, tests, change, cleanup, and verified result. Do not test production systems without authorization or put real credentials in a public portfolio.

### 3. Select training

Compare [training resources](/it-career-playbook/getting-started-in-it/it-training-resources-online/) and [degree choices](/it-career-playbook/it-certifications-and-degrees/do-i-need-a-degree-for-it/) against your five postings. If a certification fits, use the vendor's current exam objectives. [CompTIA](/it-career-playbook/it-certifications-and-degrees/comptia-certification-roadmap/), [Cisco](/it-career-playbook/it-certifications-and-degrees/cisco-networking-certifications/), and [cloud](/it-career-playbook/it-certifications-and-degrees/cloud-certifications-guide/) paths serve different work. No single sequence fits every role.

### 4. Apply with evidence

Use the [resume lesson](/it-career-playbook/getting-a-job-in-it/it-resume-writing-tips/) and [interview questions](/it-career-playbook/getting-a-job-in-it/it-interview-questions/). Name your own contribution, distinguish lab work from paid experience, and explain how you verified an outcome. Review posting instructions before submitting a file.

## Final Check

Give your target-role sheet and troubleshooting record to someone new to your lab. Ask them to identify the role you seek, reproduce the safe check, and explain your diagnosis using only your notes. Revise missing steps. You have completed this playbook's beginner path when the record is repeatable and each application claim has a matching example. Hiring outcomes depend on the employer and market.

For an existing IT role, use [advancement](/it-career-playbook/moving-up-in-it/earning-certifications-and-advancing-in-it/) and [technical versus management](/it-career-playbook/moving-up-in-it/technical-vs-management-track/) to choose a next responsibility.
